# Ticket 05: quality failures, GPS Gaps and Evidence Holes

A processed Day now separates three different situations that previously shared one "unresolved interval" concept. Missing observations are a **GPS Gap** published as a Timeline event. Observations that exist but cannot support activity are **Evidence Hole** coverage with a reason. Rejected observations are **classified** deterministically and withheld from derived geometry only, with Raw GPS untouched.

## Quality classification

`quality::classify` (recorded as `raw-quality-classification-v1`) assigns every captured observation `usable`, `low_quality` or `excluded` from its immutable Raw metadata plus the captured policy. It runs in `capture`, so a published classification is reproducible from the Raw metadata and the recorded policy alone.

Acquisition metadata that cannot support a position — `fix_quality <= 0`, no satellite in view, or HDOP above `max_hdop` — is `low_quality`. A satellite count of zero is **Raw acquisition validity**: a receiver claiming a fix with nothing in view cannot have measured a position, so the record is present but unusable rather than physically impossible. An observation is `excluded` from derived geometry when the receiver reports a speed above `max_implied_speed_mps`, or when its displacement from the previous `usable` observation exceeds `max_implied_speed_mps × elapsed` with an absolute `jump_distance_floor_m` for very short intervals. A receiver-reported speed above the limit is the **configured implied-speed limit** applied to the receiver's own claim, rather than a displacement the server derived. Both thresholds sit inside the criterion's configurable implied-speed/jump/quality policies, which is why migration `0007_quality_gaps.sql` also keys on `satellites <= 0` and `speed_mps`.

Displacement is measured from the previous *trusted* position, so an already-rejected record cannot push the next reliable record into an impossible jump of its own. The deliberate consequence is latent over-exclusion: after one impossible jump, a legitimately fast-moving Device stays `excluded` until it returns within the implied-speed budget of the stale trusted position. That trade-off is intentional, and `a_fast_moving_device_recovers_only_after_returning_near_the_trusted_position` pins it through the public Daily View: the day recovers rather than staying rejected, and the rejected records contribute no distance.

Raw GPS is never updated, deleted or rewritten. `gps_points` keeps the jump, so the Owner can still inspect what the Device recorded, and the jump contributes no Stop, Trip, Route Part, distance or daily duration.

## The three published classes

The Daily View publishes all three classes separately: `usable_point_count`, `low_quality_point_count` and `excluded_point_count`. `excluded_point_count` is the real `Excluded` count, counted from the classifier's own variants rather than derived as `point_count - usable_count`, and the three counts always partition `point_count`. The two-way count was indistinguishable in practice: a poor-HDOP record and an impossible jump both read as `excluded 1`.

## GPS Gaps

`gaps::detect` (recorded as `observed-gap-detection-v1`) reads the complete Raw series **before** quality filtering, at millisecond precision against `observation_gap_s`. A Gap never precedes the first or follows the last Raw observation, and it is never inferred from quality filtering: low-quality records at existing timestamps produce Evidence Holes instead.

A Gap is published as a `kind: "gap"` Timeline item alongside Trips and Stops, in chronological order with observed and visible bounds, `continues_before`/`continues_after` and `daily_observed_duration_s`, feeding `gap_count` and `gap_duration_s`. It ends Trip continuity, contributes only its own duration, and produces no movement, Stop, matcher call or straight Route connector. Geometry on either side stays disconnected, one Route Part per Trip.

## Evidence Holes

`holes::holes` (in `processing/holes.rs`) produces coverage only for intervals that contain Raw observations. `insufficient_quality` covers unreliable metadata, `insufficient_geometry` covers an impossible position, and `ambiguous_activity` covers reliable observations no Stop or Trip explains. The class-to-reason decision, including the stronger-claim rule that an impossible position outranks a merely poor neighbour, belongs to `quality::hole_reason`, so coverage metadata never re-derives it. Each hole publishes a projected day-clipped interval, a reason and the Raw record count, separately from Timeline events. The `unsupported_classification` reason arrives with the mode-classification slice that can actually emit it; until then it is not in the contract, because a reason no server code can publish is a promise the Owner cannot rely on. A matcher failure alone is never a hole.

The predicate that defines a GPS Gap, `gaps::absent`, is the single absence test in the crate: `holes` asks the same function rather than keeping a second copy, so a Gap and an Evidence Hole can never be derived from two disagreeing notions of absence.

Neither side of a hole is claimed to be one Trip and no Stop is inferred across it, so activity adjacent to a hole keeps `open` actual boundaries with null `actual_start_at`/`actual_end_at` and null `full_duration_s`. Nothing is extrapolated to now or to the end of the day.

## Evidence state

`evidence_state` reflects supported activity coverage only. No Trip or Stop means `insufficient`, supported activity plus Evidence Holes means `partial`, and supported activity with no unresolved coverage means `sufficient`. An explicit GPS Gap alone does not downgrade `sufficient` to `partial`: an absence is a truthful fact about the Day, not unresolved coverage. An entirely unusable day still publishes a successful empty view, and `usable_point_count + low_quality_point_count + excluded_point_count` equals `point_count`.

## Configurable policy and provenance

Migration `0007_quality_gaps.sql` adds `max_hdop`, `max_implied_speed_mps` and `jump_distance_floor_m` to `device_processing_control`, with the same `target_generation` advance trigger pattern as migration 0005, and advances the target identity to `quality-gaps-v1`. Publication built by the earlier reducer therefore reads as stale until rebuilt, and a policy change retires the current publication the same way. Each Activity Revision records the policy and the algorithm list in `config`, and snapshots report `reducer_version: 2`.

`Target` owns that policy. `Target { ..., #[sqlx(flatten)] policy: quality::Policy }` reads the configured thresholds from the same columns that select the target, so `quality::Policy::from_target` and its field-by-field copy no longer exist and no caller can hold a second copy of the policy that drifts from the captured one. `capture` classifies with `&target.policy` and `staging::config` records `target.policy.max_hdop` and its siblings; both read the same owned value.

## API and Web

The canonical contract adds the `DailyGap` schema and the `gap` activity kind, replaces `UnresolvedEvidence` with `EvidenceHole` and its reason enum, adds `low_quality_point_count` to the `DailySummary` required list, and removes `unresolved_intervals` from `DailyView`. Web types are regenerated with `npm run api:generate`.

`web/src/features/timeline/evidence.ts` owns both explanations. A Gap renders as its own Timeline item titled "GPS Gap" stating that no movement, Stop or connector is inferred. `DailyEvidenceNotice.vue` lists the Evidence Holes with their reason and Raw record count, and never claims observations are missing; above them it names the day's `low_quality_point_count` and `excluded_point_count` separately, so the corrected counts reach the Owner without new UI. The Timeline kind union gained `gap`, with a distinct marker. The Map draws each published Route Part as its own GeoJSON line, so a Gap reads as a break in the geometry, and it creates no marker per GPS Record.

`evidence.ts` keys its reason map off the generated `EvidenceHole["reason"]` union and takes `EvidenceHole` from `features/activity/model.ts`, so a reason the contract gains later fails the typecheck instead of silently falling through to a raw code.

## Boundary of this slice

No OSRM matching, mode classification, cross-midnight work, processed playback clock or incremental range reuse is included; those are tickets 06–15. A Trip ending at a GPS Gap does not confirm that boundary: the ADR-0001 Stable Segmentation Boundary rule needs evidence on both sides that only later range-expansion work establishes, so the boundary stays open rather than asserting a departure time the observations cannot prove. `gps/1` ingestion semantics and firmware are unchanged.

## Validation evidence

`processing/evidence.rs` was renamed to `processing/holes.rs`, because it contains only Evidence Holes while `gaps.rs` is named for GPS Gaps; `mod.rs`, `derived.rs` and `snapshot.rs` import the new name.

The six tests that used to live in one 541-line `quality_gap_processing.rs` are split by responsibility into `tests/quality_classification.rs` (the three published classes, configured policy, entirely unusable day, recovery after a jump — four tests) and `tests/gap_evidence.rs` (genuine absence → GPS Gap, impossible jump → no excursion, low-quality → Evidence Hole, fixture truthfulness — four tests). The shared `at(hour, minute)` clock, the JSON scenario loader and the end-to-end publish harness moved to `tests/support/` (`mod.rs` and `scenarios.rs`), and `trip_processing.rs` now uses the shared `at()` rather than its own copy.

- `gap_evidence.rs`: real HTTP upload → worker → PostGIS → Daily View reads. A genuine absence publishes `trip, gap, trip` with a 660 second Gap, no Stop, no bridging Route Part, `gap_duration_s` 660, `evidence_state: sufficient` and Raw rows identical before and after. An impossible jump keeps all seven Raw records, reports `usable 6 / low_quality 0 / excluded 1`, discloses one `insufficient_geometry` hole, keeps both Trips' boundaries open and adds no distance. Low-quality records at the *same* timestamps as the absence case produce zero Gaps, one `insufficient_quality` hole of seven records, `usable 6 / low_quality 5 / excluded 0`, two Trips and `partial` evidence.
- `gap_evidence.rs` fixtures (`fixtures/quality_gaps.json`): ten scenarios covering a genuine absence, continuous low-quality observations, one impossible jump, a Gap and a hole in one day, a poor record beside an impossible one, an entirely unusable day, ambiguous activity, poor HDOP, a fix without satellites and a receiver reporting impossible speed. Each asserts chronological kinds, Gap bounds, hole reason/bounds/record count, Trip bounds, published distance, evidence state and all three class counts, which must sum to the Raw total. The mixed scenario is what makes the stronger-claim rule load-bearing: dropping the `Excluded` short-circuit from `quality::hole_reason` merges the poor record into `insufficient_quality` and fails the fixture.
- `quality_classification.rs` three classes: one impossible jump and one run of continuous poor-quality observations produce `excluded_point_count` 1 versus 0 while `low_quality_point_count` is 0 versus 5, so the two situations are distinguishable through the public Daily View alone. Publishing `point_count - usable_count` again fails this test.
- `quality_classification.rs` recovery: an excluded record followed by real movement that stays away from the stale trusted position keeps `excluded_point_count` 2 and `usable_point_count` 4, and the rejected records contribute no distance.
- `quality_classification.rs` configurable policy: relaxing `max_hdop` reclassifies the same Raw records from `low_quality 5` into a Stop, the previous publication stays readable as `stale`, `target_generation` advances by one, and the Activity Revision's `config` records the policy plus both algorithm identities.
- Existing ticket 01–04 tests stay green, including the previously `missing_observations` assertions in `trip_processing.rs` and `stationary_fixtures.rs`, now expressed as Gap Timeline events and renamed hole reasons.
- Mutation checks: treating every observation as usable, dropping the implied-speed/jump policy, detecting Gaps only between usable observations, connecting movement across a Gap, letting a Gap downgrade evidence to `partial`, dropping the policy from provenance, removing the migration's target bump, publishing `excluded_point_count` as `point_count - usable_count` again, and dropping the stronger-claim rule each fail the suite.
- Web: `features/timeline/evidence.test.ts`, `timeline/events.test.ts`, `components/RouteMap.test.ts` and `DailyMapPage.test.ts` cover the distinct Gap and Evidence Hole explanations, the Gap Timeline item without a coordinate, disconnected Part geometry, the two withheld-coverage counts named separately, and the hole notice never claiming missing observations. A mutation that reuses the absence message for a hole fails three tests.
- Web, no marker per GPS Record: the assertion counts the GeoJSON features written into *every* source and compares them between a 30 000-record day and a 2-record day, and pins the marker-bearing sources to the published Route Parts and Stops. Adding one Point feature per `summary.point_count` in `map/route-parts.ts` fails it (features 9 and 10 where 1 and 2 are expected); the previous source-and-layer-count version passed under the same mutation.
- Contract: `openapi-typescript` regenerates cleanly, and the ten real published responses were cross-checked against the canonical `DailySummary`, `EvidenceHole` and processed-`DailyView` shape. Four deliberate mutations — a reintroduced `unsupported_classification` reason, a dropped `low_quality_point_count`, a two-way `point_count - usable_count` summary, and a hole carrying an undeclared `center` — are all rejected. No JSON Schema validator or PyYAML is available in this environment, so this cross-check reads the contract text rather than resolving `$ref`s, and it does not enforce `additionalProperties: false` on `DailyView`.
